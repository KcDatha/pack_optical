<h2>What Causes Wrinkles?</h2>

<p>Wrinkles are those fine lines and creases that show up on our skin as we age. They can appear anywhere but are especially common around the eyes, mouth, and forehead. There are <a href="https://www.healthline.com/health/beauty-skin-care/wrinkles#wrinkle-types" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">2 main types of wrinkles</a>:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Dynamic wrinkles</strong> are caused by repetitive facial movements—think smile lines or frown lines.</li>

<li><strong>Static wrinkles</strong>, on the other hand, result from factors like aging, sun damage, and lifestyle choices causing the skin to lose elasticity.</li>
</ul>

<p>Under-eye wrinkles are a type of static wrinkle. The skin around your eyes is thinner and more delicate compared to other areas of your face, making it more prone to wrinkles. The biggest factors for more noticeable under-eye wrinkles are:</p>

<h3>Aging</h3>

<p>Aging is the most common reason for under-eye wrinkles. As we age, our skin produces less <a href="https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/collagen" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">collagen</a> and elastin—proteins that keep our skin firm and elastic. This decrease in collagen leads to thinner skin and the increased formation of wrinkles.</p>

<h3>Sun Exposure</h3>

<p>Sun exposure is another major culprit behind under-eye wrinkles. UV rays from the sun can penetrate the skin and <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33980846/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">break down collagen fibers</a>, effectively accelerating the skin’s aging process. This is known as <a href="https://www.skincancer.org/blog/photoaging-what-you-need-to-know/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">photoaging</a>, and it often results in fine lines, wrinkles, and sunspots—as well as an increased risk of sun cancer.</p>

<h3>Lifestyle &amp; Environmental Factors</h3>

<p>Your lifestyle significantly affects your skin health. Here are <a href="https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/collagen#collagen-loss" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">some factors</a> that can contribute to under-eye wrinkles by causing collagen loss:</p>

<ul>
<li><a href="https://www.healthline.com/health/smoking/effects-on-body" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Smoking</a></li>

<li><a href="https://www.sleepfoundation.org/physical-health/beauty-sleep" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Lack of sleep</a></li>

<li>Poor diet high in added sugar and ultra-processed foods</li>

<li>Excessive alcohol consumption</li>

<li>Environmental factors (pollution, dry climates)</li>
</ul>

<p>While genetics does appear <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4085290/#:~:text=Recent%20studies%20on%20twins%20have%20revealed%20that%20up%20to%2060%25%20of%20the%20skin%20aging%20variation%20between%20individuals%20can%20be%20attributed%20to%20genetic%20factors%2C%20while%20the%20remaining%2040%25%20is%20due%20to%20non%2Dgenetic%20factors" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">to have some effect</a> on your wrinkles, that’s not something within your power to change. But with this knowledge, you can be extra cautious about protecting your skin and taking proactive steps to address any skin issues.</p>
